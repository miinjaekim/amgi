## The thing to decide first

**This pack sets aside the "audience is not beginners" rule.** You asked for it
that way on 2026-08-24 and I have recorded it as a deliberate exception rather
than a change of principle — the module header and the backlog entry both say
so. Everything else in the pack follows from that one call, so if you want it
back inside the rule, say so now and the list gets rebuilt around the *domain*
reading instead (everyday register a competent speaker still lacks: `security
deposit`, `co-pay`, `walk-in clinic`).

**What I did with the exception, so it does not become a bad deck.** A beginner
list fails in a specific way — it fills with the two hundred words a Korean
learner already met in middle school (`happy`, `wait`, `always`), feels
comprehensive, and teaches nothing. So the filter is **concrete over frequent**:
elementary in register, specific in reference. `faucet`, `drawer`, `leftovers`,
`errand`, `deposit`, `prescription` — the things you have to *name* to get
through a day. Bare high-frequency function words are deliberately absent; they
are learned by being used, not by being carded.

**Korean backs only.** On an English pack the study side *is* the `english`
slot, so an authored English back would be overwritten at save time and could
never be read. (Same as TOEIC — see [toeic-backs-draft.md](toeic-backs-draft.md).)

**Context hints** are on two kinds of entry only, not decoratively:
phrasal verbs where the particle *is* the meaning (`put away` ≠ `put`), and the
handful of nouns whose everyday sense is not the word's commonest one (`change`
is coins, `line` is a queue, `dishes` is the washing-up).

**No overlap with the TOEIC pack**, checked in a test rather than by eye — saved
marking is keyed on the study text, so a word in two packs would show as already
saved in the deck you never touched.

**Sources:** the everyday strands of the Oxford 3000 and the New General Service
List, filtered against 국립국어원's 한국어–영어 생활 어휘 pairings for the words
whose Korean equivalent a learner would not guess. Assembled by hand.

## Open questions for you

1. **149 is on the large side for a beginner deck.** TOEIC is 133, TOPIK 160.
   Sections are the enrolment unit so nobody has to take it all at once, but if
   you want it tighter, "Verbs you use every day" and "The shape of a day" are
   where I would cut.
2. **`brush` is glossed for two objects** (이를 닦다 / 머리를 빗다) because
   English uses one verb where Korean uses two. That is the two-gloss rule
   working, but it is the entry most likely to read as a dodge.
3. **`sink` covers 싱크대 and 세면대.** One English word, two Korean rooms. Left
   as one entry with a hint; splitting it would need two different English
   words, and there aren't two.
4. **Loanword backs are avoided** the way 장려금 was chosen over 인센티브 on
   TOEIC — but `recipe → 조리법, 레시피` keeps both, because 레시피 genuinely is
   what people say now. Flag if you want it dropped.
