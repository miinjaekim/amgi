# 군사용어 — 안보·정세 / Military Terms: Security Affairs — Draft for Review

**Proposed pack name.** `military-affairs`, shown as **Military Terms: Security
Affairs** in an English UI and **군사용어 — 안보·정세** in a Korean one. Both
sides have to be direction-neutral, because this pack runs in both directions.
안보·정세 was chosen over 시사, which is the more natural Korean for a news
register but does not cover §9 (commemoration) or §5 (ministries) — those are
standing knowledge, not current affairs.

**One of two.** This pack was split out of a single 474-term draft. Its sibling
is [군사용어 — 부대·참모](military-unit-pack-draft.md), the register of a unit
and a combined staff. The two share an audience and a card shape but almost no
vocabulary, and the split is by *register*, not difficulty — neither is the
beginner one. Cross-references below to the sibling pack are written
「부대·참모 §n」.

**Concept:** the language of a room with a microphone in it. A commander's
press engagement, a combined staff briefing, a KBS defense segment being
interpreted live, a wreath-laying with a visiting delegation. The unit of study
is still the pair, and the pack still runs **in both directions**: the same
authored list registered under English for a Korean-native learner and under
Korean for an English-native one.

**Who it's for.** 통역장교 and 통역병 assigned anywhere near public affairs,
ROK officers on a combined staff, and — reading the other way — US personnel
who have to follow a Korean-language briefing or news segment. The sibling
pack's audience is the same people on a different day.

**The register, and the failure mode.** In 부대·참모 the failure mode is
stumbling: you cannot produce 단편명령 fast enough and the moment passes. Here
it is worse. You say "joint" where the ROK–US relationship requires "combined",
or call an ADIZ entry an airspace violation, or render 전작권 이양 as a
"transfer" when the official word is "transition" — and you have made a claim
the government has not made, in front of people who will quote it. Most of
§1 is fixed formulas for exactly this reason: "ironclad" is not one adjective
among several for the US security commitment, it is *the* adjective, and any
other choice reads as a downgrade to anyone who follows the alliance.

**Selection filter:** unchanged from the sibling — terms where knowing the
concept does not give you the word. Applied to this register it cuts harder
than expected: 중동 / the Middle East, 난민 / refugee, 냉전 / the Cold War and
테러 / terrorism are all transparent and are out, while 이동식 발사대,
무해통항권, 고각 발사 and 원점타격 are the entire point. §5 is the purest case
in either pack — knowing what an agency does tells you nothing whatsoever about
what it is called in English.

**Context hints** do more work here than in the sibling, and a different kind
of work. There they warn you off a wrong word; here they usually carry the
register or the politics — which of several right answers to reach for, and
what the choice commits you to.

**Sources:** the 국문·영문 editions of 국방부's
[국방백서](https://www.mnd.go.kr) read side by side, which is where the English
names of ROK organizations and programmes come from; MND, 외교부, USFK and CFC
English-language releases; UN Security Council resolution texts for the
sanctions vocabulary; and the US
[DoD Dictionary of Military and Associated Terms](https://www.jcs.mil) for the
doctrinal half of §8. All unclassified, all published. **Assembled by hand from
those, not scraped.**

**Coverage check.** This pack exists because the original 부대·참모 draft was
reconciled against a widely circulated 어학병/통역장교 선발 preparation glossary
(359 entries, V4.16, April 2025) built for the selection exam's
news-interpretation task. The overlap with that draft was roughly fifty
concepts — under a quarter of either list — and nearly everything the glossary
covered that the draft did not is now the ten sections below. Three of its
glosses were not carried over as written: it renders 한미 연례 연합 군사연습
as a "joint military exercise" while separately listing 연합 as combined and
합동 as joint, so it contradicts itself and 부대·참모 §10 is the reason why; it
gives 지대지/지대함/지대공 as "ground-to-", where US usage is "surface-to-";
and it glosses 군무원 as "army civilians".

**Accuracy caveat, read before the list.** This pack goes stale faster than
anything else we have shipped. §4 is the worst of it — the three-axis system's
English names are set by MND and move with policy — and §1's alliance formulas
are rewritten at every summit. §7 and §9 are the stable end. A reviewer who
works a combined staff or a public affairs desk now is worth more here than
anywhere else in the app.

## 9. 역사·추모 — History, incidents and remembrance

Ceremonies are a real and frequent assignment — a wreath-laying, a visiting

## Open questions

- **Acronyms: here the acronym is the answer.** The sibling pack can tolerate
  either convention. This one cannot. SLBM, ICBM, THAAD, ADIZ, ISR, KAMD, KMPR
  and CVID are only ever said as acronyms — nobody has spoken the words "Korea
  Massive Punishment and Retaliation" aloud in a briefing, and a card whose back
  is the full expansion is testing something no interpreter is ever asked to do.
  I have written `expansion (ACRONYM)` throughout for consistency with the
  sibling, and I think that is wrong for §3 and §4. The alternative is the
  acronym alone with the expansion in `context`.
- **One term, several right answers.** This pack breaks the one-to-one
  assumption harder than the two-gloss allowance covers. 북한 is North Korea /
  the North / Pyongyang depending on what you said in the previous sentence, and
  choosing among them across a long passage is the actual skill being tested.
  I have written a single back and pushed the alternatives into the hint, which
  teaches recognition and not production. If there is a card shape that grades
  "any of these", this pack is the argument for it.
- **No usage instances.** The prep glossary this was reconciled against carries
  a real news sentence on roughly three fifths of its entries, with the term
  bolded in place. `context` here is a definition or a warning, which is better
  on a card and worse for collocation — 도발 travels with 감행하다, 태세 with
  격상하다, and nothing in this file teaches that. The gap matters more here than
  in the sibling, because this register is almost entirely collocation.
- **Proper nouns and the review answer.** 독도, 동해, 서해, 야스쿠니, 사도광산
  and the four named overseas units are facts about naming, not vocabulary, and
  a user who already knows them will find the cards trivial while a user who
  does not will find them unguessable. That is the selection filter working as
  designed, but it makes for a lumpy section.
- **Political sensitivity is in the content, not just the hints.** 동해 / the
  East Sea, 독도, 위안부 and 강제 징용 have contested English renderings, and
  the ones here are the ROK government's. That is the correct choice for a pack
  teaching a ROK interpreter what to say, and it should be a stated editorial
  position rather than something a reviewer discovers.
- **Currency, and who reviews it.** §4 will be wrong first; §1 second. See the
  caveat above.
- **`pronounceable` is yes, and the hints are carrying work they should not
  have to.** 이지스함 exists half as a pronunciation warning, and 패권
  hegemony was left out entirely because a hint column is a poor place to put a
  stress mark. An English-native learner has the harder job in this pack —
  잠수함발사 탄도미사일 out loud is not a small ask.
- **Ordering against the sibling.** These two are not a sequence — a 통역장교
  headed for public affairs wants this pack first, a 통역병 in a line unit wants
  부대·참모 first — but the deck page has no way to say "either, depending". If
  it lists them in a fixed order, that order is a recommendation whether we mean
  it as one or not.
